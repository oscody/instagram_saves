# King Njoya and the Bamum Writing System

A history post about King Njoya of the Bamum Kingdom in present-day Cameroon, covering his Shü-mom script, the Bamum Chronicle, his comic-style manuscript illustrations, and his palace designs in Foumban. It also says German colonial authorities tried to credit his work to German influence after 1916, and shows manuscript pages, the new palace, and a man speaking to camera.

## Caption

King Njoya of the Bamum Kingdom was more than a ruler he was a visionary.

He created an entire writing system called the Shü-mom script, which evolved from over 500 symbols into a refined phonetic script. He documented his people’s history, contributing to the Great Chronicle of the Bamum Empire. He was also an artist, leaving behind comic-style illustrations in Bamum manuscripts.

And his genius didn’t stop there he designed his own palaces in Foumban, including the modern palace.

When Germans arrived and took control of the region in 1916, some tried to claim his brilliance came from German influence. But King Njoya stood firm: this was  native Bamum innovation, African creativity, African excellence.

This is the story they don’t teach enough.
