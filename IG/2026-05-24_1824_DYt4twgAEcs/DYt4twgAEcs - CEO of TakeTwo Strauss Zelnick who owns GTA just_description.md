# Take-Two CEO: AI builds assets, not hits

A carousel quoting Take-Two CEO Strauss Zelnick from a podcast interview, arguing that AI is strong at producing game assets but cannot create hits, because data is backward-looking while creativity is forward-looking. It adds that clones don't sell and that real hits are unexpected.

Written by claude-haiku-5-5.

## Caption

CEO of Take-Two Strauss Zelnick, who owns GTA, just made one of the clearest arguments yet against the idea that AI will eventually replace creative studios.

On David Senra's (@davidsenra) podcast, Strauss Zelnick said he is "all in" on AI as a productivity tool, but rejected the idea that it can create the next blockbuster on its own. AI is built on data sets, and data sets are backward looking.

Creativity is forward looking. That gap is why AI can produce assets fast, but cannot produce hits.

Zelnick made a distinction between asset creation and hit creation. AI can generate textures, models, and lines of code in seconds. Assets alone do not build a cultural phenomenon.

They are a necessary condition for a hit, not a sufficient one.

He also dismissed the fear that AI lowers the barrier to game creation. Thousands of mobile games launch annually, and only a handful become hits.

The bottleneck has never been tools. It has been originality. And clones, he said, do not sell.
