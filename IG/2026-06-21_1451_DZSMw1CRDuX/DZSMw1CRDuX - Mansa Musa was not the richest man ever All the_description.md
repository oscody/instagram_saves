# Tunka Manin and the wealth of Ancient Ghana

The post argues that Mansa Musa was not the richest man ever and that earlier rulers of Ancient Ghana, especially Tunka Manin, were wealthier but less known to Europe. It backs this with claims about Ghana's gold trade, Muhammad ibn Hawqal's 988 remark, and details of Tunka Manin's reign and lifestyle.

## Caption

Mansa Musa was not the richest man ever. All the kings of Ancient Ghana who reigned over the last thousand years would qualify to be richer. Because Tunka Manin is the last know ruler of Ancient Ghana, you can make the case, he is the last known richest person ever. Just because he didn’t take a trip for Arabs to see, doesn’t make Mansa any richer.

All Mansa Musa did was inherit the land from his great grandfather, who conquered ancient Ghana in the year 1240. It’s the people who existed before the Mali empire existed, who are the real royal kings and queens the history books don’t have enough information about.

Mansa Musa is popular, because he made contact with the Middle East, and in return, Europe found out about him. We know about him, because they know about him. Everyone who was a king in Ancient Ghana before Mansa, was the richest man who ever lived.

Only difference is no European promotion of Ancient Ghana being the richest civilization post the Arab conquest.

Another Arab writer, the geographer Muhammad ibn Hawqal, wrote in 988 that the ruler of Ghana was the richest king in the world. 
Around 1068 in the pre-colonial era, Ghana was clearly the largest, wealthiest and most powerful state in West Africa if not in Africa. There were two important reasons for this success. First, the discovery of large quantities of gold made it possible for the Ghana Empire to trade with North African merchants and other traders of ‘white’ origin who provided horses, salt and iron implements in exchange for gold.

Tunka Manin or Tunkamenin, born around 1010 AD, was a ruler of the Ghana Empire who reigned from 1062 to 1076 AD. His predecessor was King Bassi. He had obtained the kingship because he was the son of the former ruler’s sister as the Soninke followed the matrilineal system of inheritance. Tunkamenin commanded great devotion from his subjects and respect from foreign visitors. He was a powerful ruler controlling some 200,000 warriors and surrounded himself with an air of divinity. Apart from this, he was extremely wealthy as he wore exquisite cloths made of silk and cotton, and he decked himself in gold and ivory.

Problems in the post: - Mansa Musa (reigned about 1312–1337) did not inherit from a great-grandfather who conquered Ghana in 1240. Sundiata Keita defeated the Sosso at Kirina around 1235. Ghana had already declined by then, and the Mali Empire absorbed its old territory. - Musa is known mainly through his 1324 hajj and Arab and later European accounts. That is a record of fame, not a ranking of wealth. - No source lets anyone rank "richest ever". Ghana's wealth was real, but rulers of that era can't be compared by net worth. - Ghana's end is dated to the Almoravid conquest around 1076, but historians dispute how much the Almoravids actually did.
