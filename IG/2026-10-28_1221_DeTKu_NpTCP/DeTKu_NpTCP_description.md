# Anatomy of a wave for beginner surfers

Drone footage of breaking waves, labelled with wall/face, shoulder and whitewash. The caption defines nine wave parts and says knowing them helps with positioning, timing and wave selection.

Written by claude-haiku-5-5.

## What it teaches

1. Peak: the highest point of the wave, where it begins to break.
2. Lip: the top edge that pitches forward as the wave breaks.
3. Face / wall: the clean, unbroken section you ride along.
4. Shoulder: the softer, less powerful section farther from the peak.
5. Impact zone: where the breaking lip crashes into the water.
6. Whitewater: the foamy water left behind after a wave breaks.
7. Pocket: the steep, powerful section closest to where the wave is breaking.
8. Barrel: the hollow tube created when the lip throws over the face.
9. Closeout: a wave that breaks all at once, leaving little or no open face to ride.
10. Use these terms to work out where to sit, when to paddle and which waves to catch.

## Caption

When you’re learning to surf, one of the first things you need to understand is the anatomy of a wave. 🌊

Knowing how to identify different parts of a wave will help you improve your positioning, timing and wave selection.

Here’s a quick breakdown:

🌊 **Peak** – The highest point of the wave where it begins to break.

🌊 **Lip** – The top edge of the wave that pitches forward as it breaks.

🌊 **Face / Wall** – The clean, unbroken section of the wave you ride along.

🌊 **Shoulder** – The softer, less powerful section farther from the peak.

🌊 **Impact Zone** – Where the breaking lip crashes into the water.

🌊 **Whitewater** – The foamy water left behind after a wave breaks.

🌊 **Pocket** – The steep, powerful section closest to where the wave is breaking.

🌊 **Barrel** – The hollow tube created when the lip throws over the face.

🌊 **Closeout** – When a wave breaks all at once, leaving little or no open face to ride.

The better you understand how waves break, the easier it becomes to recognise where to sit, when to paddle and which waves to catch.

**Learn to read the ocean, not just ride it. 🤙**
