# Benin Kingdom, 1897 raid and looted bronzes

A reel retelling the 1897 British attack on the Kingdom of Benin, the looting of its bronzes, and the bronzes' current holdings, including about 928 in the British Museum and a 2016 private sale for ten million pounds. It is framed by a woman speaking in a Benin-style setting, with images of bronze casting and a museum gallery.

## Caption

Arrived in the Kingdom of Benin. Crashed the holiest week of the year. Got marched out by festival guards. Watched a British official get told the same thing and decide the rules did not apply to him...

Comment ‘BENIN’ for sources and further reading.
Benin City. 1897. Palm oil lamps. Houses with no doors because theft was essentially unknown.
Their history was cast in brass (Benin Bronzes) on the palace walls. Not decorations. Books.

The walls took 150 million hours to build. By hand. Guinness World Record: longest earthworks ever built before the mechanical age.

James Phillips was told to wait two months. He came anyway. A month later Britain sent 1,200 soldiers. They burned the city, took thousands of bronzes and sold them to pay for the trip.

One sold in 2016 for ten million pounds.

Nine hundred and twenty eight are in the British Museum. A British law from 1963 says they cannot be returned. Benin has been asking since 1935.

You probably were not taught this. Now you are.
