# AT&T grant essay tips for small businesses

The reel shows the details of a $50K AT&T small business grant: the requirements and the two essay prompts. It closes with a paid essay review offer.

Written by claude-haiku-5-5.

## What it teaches

1. Essay #1: open with the problem your business exists to solve, before introducing yourself or how long you've been in business.
2. Essay #1: get specific on impact with real numbers, names, and stories instead of vague claims like "I help my community."
3. Essay #1: give every dollar a destination and a result, stating what it pays for and what outcome it produces.
4. Essay #2: read AT&T's "Connecting Changes Everything" as being about what your business connects people to, not about internet or technology.
5. Essay #2: show what changes in someone's life because of the connection your business creates.

## Caption

Planning to apply for the AT&T grant? I got you 📝

Essay #1 — most people will open with their name and how long they’ve been in business. *DON’T*

Open with the problem you exist to solve so the judge feels it before you introduce yourself. 

Then get specific on impact (numbers, names, real stories  NOT *I help my community*)

And give every dollar a destination AND a result. NOT *I’d use it to grow*

Instead: $25K to launch evening and weekend classes so working parents can finally attend because we currently turn away 30 families a month for lack of spots; $15K toward a bilingual instructor to reach the Spanish-speaking families who make up 40% of our waitlist; $10K for transportation stipends for 75 families so cost is never the reason a child misses a session.

Every dollar has a job, a number, and an outcome. (Use real numbers from YOUR business!!! Funders trust specific over impressive.)

Essay #2 — this is the one people will overthink. AT&T brand promise *Connecting Changes Everything* is NOT about internet or technology. It’s about what your business connects people to.

Example: a children’s bookstore doesn’t just sell books — it connects kids to characters who look like them, to a lifelong love of reading, to confidence. 

That’s the angle. Show what changes in someone’s life because of the connection you create.
