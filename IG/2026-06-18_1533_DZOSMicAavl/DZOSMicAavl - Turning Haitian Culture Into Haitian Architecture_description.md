# Turning Haitian Culture Into Haitian Architecture

The reel proposes a neighborhood design for Haiti built around the lakou (courtyard), with walls that record history, art built into the structure, and spaces meant for ceremony and connection. The frames are rendered neighborhood views labeled "Living Lakou" and "Living Art," plus a decorative iron gate with painted murals.

Written by claude-haiku-5-5.

## What it teaches

1. Treat the lakou as a network of relationships, not just a courtyard, and organize homes around shared communal space.
2. Organize whole neighborhoods as clusters of lakous, each containing homes, gathering spaces, gardens and courtyards.
3. Run continuous inscriptions along the walls at about four feet above ground to record families, neighborhood events, traditions, proverbs and local language.
4. Let walls act as an archive so people learn history by moving through the neighborhood.
5. Build art into the design from the start, using screens, walls, painted patterns, gates and courtyards.
6. Design spaces for gathering, reflection, celebration, ceremony and connection, not only function.
