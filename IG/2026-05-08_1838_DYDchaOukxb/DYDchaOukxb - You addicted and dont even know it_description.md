# Three supplements for smoking cravings and dependency

A man speaks from a car about smoking habits such as wraps, spliffs, woods and roba, and says many people mix nicotine into them without realising. He names three supplements for dependency patterns and cravings, each with a daily or as-needed dose.

Written by claude-haiku-5-5.

## What it teaches

1. Take 400 mg of magnesium glycinate daily to calm the nervous system and reduce the feeling that you need to smoke to relax.
2. Take 100 to 200 mg of the second supplement (transcribed as 'althenein') whenever you feel heightened stress or anxiety, to take the edge off without sedation.
3. Take 600 mg of NAC daily to help reduce the body's signals that make you feel you need to smoke, since it is described as one of the most studied supplements for addictive and compulsive behaviours.

## Research

* Verdict: Partly valid. The post is a supplement tip list with no single resource. NAC has small, mixed trials for smoking cessation, and the doses given are lower than those trialled. Magnesium glycinate and L-theanine (likely what was transcribed as "althenein") have no solid evidence for reducing smoking cravings.
* Checked: 2026-10-22 by claude-sonnet-5-5

### Resources

* [NAC 2400 mg twice daily smoking cessation RCT (Indonesia) (ncbi.nlm.nih.gov)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10944675/): paper, found by search, not opened, medium confidence. Small positive trial of NAC for quitting smoking, at far higher doses than the 600 mg in the post; seen in search results only, not opened.
* [NAC for early and end-of-treatment abstinence in cigarette smokers (dc.etsu.edu)](https://dc.etsu.edu/etsu-works/9539): paper, found by search, not opened, medium confidence. Trial at 1200 mg twice daily found no benefit over placebo; seen in search results only, not opened.

### Not found

The second supplement was not named clearly in the transcript. I assumed L-theanine and did not check it. I did not open the trial papers or look for a Cochrane review. The post's claim that many people unknowingly mix nicotine into wraps or spliffs was not checked. Not medical advice: NAC and the other supplements are not established quit-smoking treatments, so ask a clinician about proven options.
