## Caption

The jobs most likely to stay valuable in an AI-driven world may be the ones that exist in the physical world. 🤖🔧

AI can write code, analyze data, and generate designs, but it can’t simply walk into a factory, diagnose a hardware failure, replace a damaged component, or get a broken network running again.

That’s why fields like robotics, embedded systems, electrical engineering, networking, cybersecurity, and field service still rely heavily on skills that are difficult to automate completely.

AI will change how these jobs are done, but often as a tool rather than a full replacement. Judgment, troubleshooting, testing, and the ability to understand how software interacts with real machines still matter.

Semiconductor engineering is a great example. As AI infrastructure expands, so does the need for chips, data centers, power systems, networks, cooling, and specialized hardware, along with the people who design, build, maintain, and repair them.
