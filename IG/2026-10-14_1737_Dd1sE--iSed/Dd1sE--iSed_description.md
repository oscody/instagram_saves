## Caption

One of the greatest African films, Teza (2008), is an Ethiopian historical drama directed by Haile Gerima. 

The film won the top prize at the 2009 Pan African Film and Television Festival of Ouagadougou (FESPACO), Africa’s most prestigious film festival. won the Jury Prize and Best Screenplay at the Venice Film Festival. It explores memory, exile, revolution, and the layers of violence.

Haile Gerima understood early that cinema could be a weapon, a mirror, or a lie. In the films he grew up watching, Black people were often flattened, distorted, or erased. So he spent his life building another kind of image, one rooted in memory, community, and return.

From Bush Mama to Harvest: 3000 Years, his films pushed back against Hollywood’s narrow scripts for Black filmmakers, refusing stereotype for observation and political clarity.

With Sankofa, he carried the film city by city. With Mypheduh, he built a shield for culture. His work reenforces strongly why it is important we tell our own stories.
