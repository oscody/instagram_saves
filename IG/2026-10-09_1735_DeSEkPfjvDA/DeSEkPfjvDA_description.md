# Decompilation methods and their legal framing

A woman reviews several decompilation approaches (matching decompilation, functional reconstruction, static recompilation, engine re-implementation, porting recovered source), citing an AI write-up for a comparison table and a U.S. copyright explanation. She says engine re-implementation and functional reconstruction are the legal ones, and that output-matching is how you check progress.

Written by claude-haiku-5-5.

## What it teaches

1. Know the categories: matching decompilation, functional reconstruction, static recompilation, engine re-implementation, and porting recovered source.
2. Treat engine re-implementation and functional reconstruction as the approaches the post calls legal; it labels the others as not legal or not really legal.
3. Judge a rewrite by comparing its output to the original, such as the pixels the GPU outputs during play.
4. Iterate against that output comparison until the rewrite matches.
5. The post's legal reasoning: U.S. copyright separates protected expression from ideas, procedures, and methods of operation, so functional information can be unprotected while program code contains protected expression (citing Sections 102 and 106 as the starting point).
