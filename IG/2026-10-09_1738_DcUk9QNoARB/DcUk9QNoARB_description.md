# Pacing in fiction: pressure, not speed

A spoken story about a narrator who learns her best friend is cheating on her husband and stays silent as the husband plans a vow renewal she agrees to attend. The speaker then uses it to explain pacing in fiction.

Written by claude-haiku-5-5.

## What it teaches

1. A faster story is not a tenser story; tension does not come from activity.
2. Readers respond to consequence: the sense that waiting is making something worse.
3. Each scene where the decision stays unmade makes the future heavier.
4. Hold the character in place, knowing what needs to happen, while the conditions around them tighten.
5. Tense pacing comes from the distance between knowing and doing, and the cost of the unresolved decision.
6. Great pacing is pressure, not speed; the pressure does not need to force the decision, only make waiting more dangerous.
