# Inbound vs. outbound job hunting for students

A professor contrasts "outbound" job hunting (applying to posted jobs and relying on luck) with "inbound" hunting, where companies come to you. He gives three examples of building visible value: contributing to open source projects, starting a newsletter or podcast, and joining a campus research lab.

Written by claude-haiku-5-5.

## What it teaches

1. Outbound job hunting means waiting for a posted job and submitting a resume, which competes with hundreds or thousands of applicants and depends on luck.
2. Aim for inbound job hunting instead, where companies chase you by demonstrating the value you can bring.
3. Pick the industry you want to enter and contribute visibly to it, for example by working on open source projects sponsored by companies you want to work for.
4. Alternatively, start a newsletter or podcast in your target industry and build an audience over a few years.
5. If you're interested in a field like healthcare, find a campus lab doing relevant research and join it, so that companies in that field are likely to find you.

## Caption

When you submit a resume, you’re basically relying on luck. The better way to get a job is to find ways of demonstrating the value you’re able to bring a potential employer so that companies start chasing you.
