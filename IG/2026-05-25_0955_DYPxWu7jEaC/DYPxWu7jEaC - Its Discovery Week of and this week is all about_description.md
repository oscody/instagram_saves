# African Myth and Folklore Fantasy Book Recommendations

A Read Africa Month Discovery Week carousel, curated by @whatrosemaryreads, listing fantasy series rooted in African myth and folklore. Each slide shows the covers and a short blurb for Tomi Adeyemi's Legacy of Orisha, Ayana Gray's Beasts of Prey, Namina Forna's The Gilded Ones, Natasha Bowen's Of Mermaids and Orisha, and Evan Winter's The Burning.

## Caption

It’s Discovery Week of #ReadAfricaMonth and this week is all about discovering new genres, authors, and books. To make that possible, I had to turn to the bookish community.

#ReadAfricaMonth has never been an individual venture; it’s a community one. I’m only one person, and there are only so many books one person can read, recommend, and speak on meaningfully. There are entire genres I rarely read (fantasy, sci-fi, poetry, graphic novels ) and because of that, there’s so much I’m still discovering too.

So this week, I’ll be highlighting genres I don’t typically read through creators who do. They’ll be sharing recommendations from across African literature, and I truly hope you follow them for even more incredible African lit recs.

Today’s feature is the wonderful Rosemary of @whatrosemaryreads whose support for African literature is genuinely unmatched.

Today, she’s sharing fantasy books rooted in African mythology and folklore.

We grow up constantly hearing about Greek mythology. We read it in schools, see it in films, and encounter it everywhere in popular culture. African mythology, however, is far less visible despite the richness and depth of stories across the continent.

Through this campaign, we hope to highlight just how expansive African storytelling truly is and to make more room for stories inspired by African myth, folklore, history, and imagination.
