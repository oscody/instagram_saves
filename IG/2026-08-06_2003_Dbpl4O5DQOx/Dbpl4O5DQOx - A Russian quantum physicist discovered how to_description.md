# Vadim Zeland's Reality Transurfing: 3-Step Method

The carousel presents Vadim Zeland's Reality Transurfing idea that reality consists of parallel tracks and that your inner state determines which one you experience. It frames a "no big deal" attitude toward goals as a way to reduce resistance.

Written by claude-haiku-5-5.

## What it teaches

1. Lower the importance of a desire: treat it as "nice to have" rather than "must have."
2. Act as if it is already arranged, moving through your day assuming the outcome has been handled.
3. When obstacles appear, mentally step sideways into a parallel reality where they don't exist.
4. Don't feed problems with attention or emotional reactions; step aside mentally instead of fighting them.
5. Avoid pretending you don't care while secretly being attached; be genuinely at peace.
6. Don't use this approach as another technique to force results; actually let go.
7. Keep taking action, but let it come from relaxation rather than desperation.

## Caption

A Russian quantum physicist discovered how to slide between parallel realities

In 1993, Vadim Zeland quit his job after a profound insight that reality exists as infinite parallel tracks, like movie versions already filmed, and that we move between them by changing our inner state. His idea became known as Reality Transurfing.

He said the harder you want something, the further it gets pushed away. Treating goals like “no big deal” works better than obsessing over them.

According to him, assigning high importance creates “excess potential,” a form of mental and emotional pressure that leads to resistance and obstacles. By lowering importance, acting calmly, and adjusting your response to problems, he believed people could move toward versions of life where things flow more easily.

A calm state improves focus and decision-making, while desperation leads to stress and mistakes.

What you chase emotionally creates tension, but what you allow comes closer. Reality does not change, but how you move within it does.

### Key steps

1. Lower the importance of the goal: move from 'must have' to 'nice to have'.
2. Drop urgency and desperation, because Zeland calls the resulting tension 'excess potential'.
3. Act and decide from a calm, neutral state.
4. Don't fight or feed problems. Step aside so the 'pendulum' loses energy.
5. Allow what you want instead of chasing it.
