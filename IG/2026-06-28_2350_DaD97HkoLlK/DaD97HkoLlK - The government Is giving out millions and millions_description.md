# SBA 7A loan steps for buying a business

A business broker's carousel describing the SBA 7A program, which it says offers up to $5,000,000 to business buyers. It lists what the buyer needs and what the loan process involves.

Written by claude-haiku-5-5.

## What it teaches

1. Find a business that has clean tax returns, a minimum 10-year lease, and 2 years of financials.
2. Make sure the buyer has business experience and a business plan.
3. Expect to put down usually 10-40 percent.
4. Be prepared that a life insurance policy and assets to pledge are sometimes required.
5. Expect the process to take 60-120 days.
6. Expect 7A loans to be variable, at prime +1-3.

## Research

* Verdict: Partly valid. SBA 7(a) is real, covers buying a business, and caps loans at $5M. The post is a lead-gen pitch ("comment SBA"). Its other figures are broker estimates, and "the government is giving out loans" is misleading, because SBA only guarantees loans that private lenders make.
* Checked: 2026-10-22 by claude-sonnet-5-5

### Resources

* [SBA 7(a) loans (sba.gov)](https://www.sba.gov/funding-programs/loans/7a-loans): page, found by search, verified, high confidence. The official page states a $5 million maximum and lists change of ownership as an eligible use. You apply through a lender, not SBA.

### Key steps

1. Gather documents: 2 years of tax returns and financials for the target business, plus your business plan and experience.
2. Use SBA Lender Match on sba.gov, or a local SBA District Office, to find a participating lender.
3. Apply directly to the lender, not to SBA.
4. Ask the lender about the down payment (the post says 10-40%), collateral, any life insurance requirement, and the variable rate over prime.

### Not found

SBA's page doesn't confirm the post's down payment range, 60-120 day timeline, 10-year lease, or prime +1-3 rate. These look like typical lender practice, not SBA rules. The broker's lenders are only available by commenting "SBA".
