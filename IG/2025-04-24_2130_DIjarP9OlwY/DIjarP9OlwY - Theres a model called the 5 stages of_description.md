# The 5 stages of relationships

A reel presenting a five-stage model of romantic relationships, with on-screen labels for red flags, stage three and stage five over couple and solo footage. The narration says most people never get past stage two.

Written by claude-haiku-5-5.

## What it teaches

1. Stage 1, honeymoon: chemistry is high and red flags are hard to see; you bond over similarities and idealize the other person.
2. Stage 2, power struggle: differences surface, you start personalizing small things, and couples often fight for control or break up and repeat stage 1 with someone new.
3. Stage 3, adaptation and acceptance: accept that the other person may not change, ask whether you can love them anyway, and use your differences to build something together. This takes discipline rather than dopamine.
4. Stage 4, commitment: stay in the relationship to build a real future and foundation, not out of convenience or conditions.
5. Stage 5, oneness: two people who understand each other deeply, built through walking through difficulty together rather than chasing early excitement.

## Caption

There’s a model called the 5 stages of relationships—most people never make it past stage 2. Here’s why:

Stage 1 is the honeymoon. Dopamine’s flying. You’re in love with the idea of them. Not them. Stage 2 is the power struggle. Triggers hit. Childhood wounds surface. Now it’s real. Most people run.

Stage 3 is adaption and acceptance. You stop trying to change them—and start changing your patterns. Here’s where most people tap out.

Stage 4 is commitment. Love becomes a choice, not a mood. Stage 5 is co-creation, oneness. You build a mission, a family, a legacy and that takes discipline, not dopamine.
