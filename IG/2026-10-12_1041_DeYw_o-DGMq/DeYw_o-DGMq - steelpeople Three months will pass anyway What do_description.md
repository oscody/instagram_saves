# Three-Month Notebook Self-Reflection Plan

A carousel that walks through a notebook-based self-reflection exercise, followed by a three-month plan to cut distractions, build a routine, and set bigger goals, with a review every 15 days.

Written by claude-haiku-5-5.

## What it teaches

1. Get a black notebook and a pen, then find a quiet moment alone to write.
2. Write honestly about where you are now: your finances, emotional state and physical state, what you do every day, what you do just to look busy, and what you are putting off.
3. Write where you should be: how much you should be earning, what your daily routine should look like, who you should have become by now, and what level of discipline you should have.
4. Write what got you off track, without blaming anyone: the decisions that brought you here, the patterns you keep repeating, how you got in your own way, where you showed weakness, and what you stopped doing.
5. Month 1: cut out what drags you down, such as excessive social media use, bad habits, or time with people who drain you. Wake up at the same time every day and complete one task each day.
6. Month 2: build a simple morning and evening routine with daily commitments you don't negotiate, and keep the promises you make to yourself.
7. Month 3: set more challenging goals in finances, productivity and personal growth, and take on more responsibility and action.
8. Every 15 days, revisit your notes: what worked, where you fell short, and what needs to change.

## Caption

steel_people_ Three months will pass anyway. What do you want to change in that time?

Grab a notebook and write honestly: where you are now, what kind of life you want, and what’s holding you back. The more specific your answers, the clearer your next step will be.

Spend the first month cutting out what you don’t need. Use the second to build a routine and learn to keep the promises you make to yourself. In the third, set more challenging goals and start taking action at a new level.

Every 15 days, revisit your notes: what worked, where you fell short, and what needs to change. This will help you see your progress and adjust your plan along the way.
