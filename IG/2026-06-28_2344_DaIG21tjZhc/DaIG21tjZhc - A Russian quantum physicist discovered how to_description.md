# Vadim Zeland's Reality Transurfing: a 3-step method

A carousel introducing Vadim Zeland and his Reality Transurfing idea that reality consists of parallel tracks and that inner state determines which one you experience. It presents his "no big deal" approach to goals, the excess potential and pendulum concepts, and a three-step method.

Written by claude-haiku-5-5.

## What it teaches

1. Lower the importance of a desire: treat it as a "nice to have" rather than a "must have" to release pressure.
2. Act as if the desire is already arranged, going through the day as if the universe has already handled it.
3. When obstacles appear, mentally step sideways into a parallel reality where they don't exist.
4. When a problem appears, don't fight it or give it attention; step aside mentally and stop feeding it energy.
5. Avoid pretending not to care while secretly being attached, avoid using the approach as another way to force results, and take action from relaxation rather than desperation.

## Caption

A Russian quantum physicist discovered how to slide between parallel realities

In 1993, Vadim Zeland quit his job after a profound insight that reality exists as infinite parallel tracks, like movie versions already filmed, and that we move between them by changing our inner state. His idea became known as Reality Transurfing.

He said the harder you want something, the further it gets pushed away. Treating goals like “no big deal” works better than obsessing over them.

According to him, assigning high importance creates “excess potential,” a form of mental and emotional pressure that leads to resistance and obstacles. By lowering importance, acting calmly, and adjusting your response to problems, he believed people could move toward versions of life where things flow more easily.

A calm state improves focus and decision-making, while desperation leads to stress and mistakes.

What you chase emotionally creates tension, but what you allow comes closer. Reality does not change, but how you move within it does.

### Key steps

1. Lower the importance of the goal; hold it lightly (the book's remedy for excess potential).
2. Act calmly and allow the outcome instead of chasing it.
3. Spot pendulums (collective beliefs and trends) that feed on your attention and emotion.
4. Don't fight or feed a pendulum; step aside mentally.
5. Keep a calm, neutral inner state to choose your 'track'.
