# Buying Retiring Boomers' Businesses With SBA Loans

A six-slide carousel claiming the U.S. government backs SBA loans that let ordinary Americans buy small businesses from retiring owners with little money down. It is built around the author's own example of buying a business with an SBA loan, investor equity and a hired manager.

Written by claude-haiku-5-5.

## What it teaches

1. Look for a boring, already profitable business whose owner is retiring.
2. Apply for an SBA 7(a) loan, which the post says can cover up to 90% of the price, since the government repays 75% of a failed loan to the bank.
3. Cover the 10% down payment with an investor or the seller.
4. Have a 680+ credit score and some management experience.
5. Hire a general manager, paid by the business, to handle staff, customers and daily operations.
6. Review the numbers monthly; the post says done this way it takes about 20 hours a week.

## Research

* Verdict: Partly valid. SBA 7(a) loans are real and can fund business acquisitions with about 10% down. But the post's figures are wrong (the cap is $5M, not $10M, and the guarantee is 75–85%, not 90%). It is also a lead-gen funnel (DM "SMB") with no public resource.

### Key steps

1. Find a profitable business with a retiring owner, using a broker listing or direct outreach.
2. Get the seller's financials and have the price and cash flow checked.
3. Ask an SBA-preferred lender about a 7(a) acquisition loan. The cap is $5M, and the guarantee is 75–85%.
4. Plan for a minimum 10% equity injection of total project cost. A seller note counts only in part, and only if it is on full standby.
5. Expect to pledge collateral on all available business assets for loans over $50k, and to give a personal guarantee.
6. Hire or retain a manager, and check that the cash flow covers the debt service plus the manager's pay.
