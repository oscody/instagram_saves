# The psychology behind why you're not consistent

A whiteboard-and-voiceover reel arguing that forcing a habit with discipline runs out, and that consistency comes from identity, emotional attachment and a nervous system that feels safe with the new behavior. It contrasts a "force" column (willpower, pressure, shame, burnout, resistance) with an "alignment" column.

Written by claude-haiku-5-5.

## What it teaches

1. Notice that forcing a habit with willpower, pressure, shame, burnout or resistance is like fueling a car with gasoline: it always runs out.
2. Recognise that your identity is built from reinforced beliefs about yourself and their corresponding values, and that these drive your behavior.
3. Form new beliefs and new values so they override old beliefs and replace old values.
4. Emotionally attach to the new behavior, because attaching gives it meaning, which becomes the cause for the behavior.
5. Create safe somatic signals in the body so the nervous system registers the new behavior as safe.
6. Repeat the behavior until it becomes automatic, focused and normal, feeling natural in the body.
7. When the behavior passes the nervous system's safety check, there is no friction, and consistency follows naturally.
