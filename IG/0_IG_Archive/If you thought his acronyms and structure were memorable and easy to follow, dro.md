When you're asked an unexpected question, it's easy to be caught off guard and start to ramble. The audience are unlikely to get your point and see how it even relates to the question.

You'll feel desperate to clarify and ramble some more. The audience will be more confused and after, upon reflection, you'll realise you didn't clearly articulate your point at all. 

The reason you ramble is because your speech is unorganised. It's hard to follow because it doesn't have any structure. It's going nowhere.

Structure makes your speech easy to understand, memorable and credible. 

In this clip from @thedesirabletruth, the speaker @michael_barayev  tells us what he's going to tell us: "C.H.A.I.R.S." and "C.O.U.P.L.E." He then explains the acronym and gives examples. 

His opening line was like a signpost of what to expect and then he met our expectations, making his answer credible, coherent and memorable, irrespective if you agree or not. 

Polish your speech by organising your answers into structures that are easy to follow, giving you credibility and coherence. Acronyms are a useful tool for memorability.
