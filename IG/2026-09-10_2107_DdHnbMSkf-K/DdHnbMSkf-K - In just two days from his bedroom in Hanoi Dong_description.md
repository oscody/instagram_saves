# Flappy Bird's developer who deleted his hit

A six-slide carousel telling the story of Dong Nguyen, who built Flappy Bird in two days in Hanoi, watched it go viral and earn about $50,000 a day from ads, then removed it at its peak. It is a narrative history, not a how-to.

## Caption

In just two days, from his bedroom in Hanoi, Dong Nguyen created a game that would become a global obsession: Flappy Bird.

He released it for free in 2013 with no publisher, marketing campaign, or big launch. Then the internet took over. Players couldn’t stop playing, restarting, raging, and challenging friends to beat their scores.

Within months, Flappy Bird reached No. 1 in the US, surpassed 50 million downloads, and was reportedly making Nguyen around $50,000 a day from ads.

But the success quickly became overwhelming. Reporters showed up at his family home, his parents discovered his fame through the news, and players began telling him they were addicted to the game.

At the height of its success, Nguyen made an unbelievable choice: he deleted it.

Millions rushed to download the game one last time before it disappeared. Nguyen walked away from tens of thousands of dollars in daily revenue, but gained something he valued more: relief.
